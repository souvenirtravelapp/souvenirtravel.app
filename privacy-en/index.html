Privacy policy

Last updated: 3 September 2026 · Publisher: Tariq Almalki · Contact: support@souvenirtravel.app

In two paragraphs

Without an account: Souvenir works completely. Everything it records — your trips, the people you travelled with, your name and picture, copies of your trip photographs, the destinations you liked — is kept on your device and in your own iCloud, in an area only your Apple Account can open. We cannot read it, and no one but you can. In this state no record of yours reaches our servers, and neither does your name or your email address — what happens is downloading pictures and data, the way any page on the internet is downloaded.

With an account: when you choose to find your trips on all your devices and in the browser, you sign in with Google or Apple. Then — and only then — Souvenir begins keeping a copy of your record in a Google database that we run. This page tells you exactly what is kept there, who reads it, and how you erase it.

Finding your next destination

The destination data Souvenir searches — months, climate, visa requirements, direct flights — is bundled inside the app itself. Searching and filtering happen entirely on your device, and the text of your search is sent nowhere.

What the app reads

Your photo library. With your permission, Souvenir reads each photograph's date and place to work out the journeys you took. This reading happens on your device. Your photographs are not sent to us or to anyone else.

Your originals in Apple Photos are never modified, moved or deleted. Removing a photograph from a trip in Souvenir leaves the original untouched.

"Your own iCloud" means the private database of this app's iCloud container, inside your Apple Account. Apple provides it; the developer has no ability to read, export or browse its contents. It counts against your own storage.

Your photographs are not uploaded to our servers — neither your trip photographs nor pictures of your companions. Videos are not copied at all: they play from Apple Photos where they are.

The account and syncing

When you sign in we use Google's Firebase for two things: confirming it is you, and keeping your record. The database is hosted in a data centre in Dammam, and the access rules are written so that no user can open anyone else's space.

Plainly, this is what reaches Google's servers from you:

  1. Your email address and your name — from Google or Apple when you sign in. Your password stays with them; we never see it and do not want it.
  2. An identifier of yours, which is the address of your space in the database.
  3. Your companions' names and how they relate to you — so your trips appear complete on your other devices.
  4. Approximate locations of the cities you visited, deliberately rounded to about a kilometre — enough to draw the map of your trips, not enough to point at a particular place.
  5. What you typed yourself: your trip notes, your travel papers, and your passport nationality.
  6. Your preferences: favourites and their months, airports, and upcoming trips.

In addition to the above, Firebase's identity service may process — for account security and abuse prevention — the internet address you signed in from, an app installation identifier, and diagnostic data about the device. Google collects these as the operator of that service, and its own policies govern them.

Our commitment: we do not sell your data, we do not share it with any third party other than Google, which hosts the database for us, and we use it for nothing except making syncing work for you. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking — not in the app and not on the website.

When does the app contact Firebase? If an account has never been used on this installation, it does not contact Firebase at all until you touch the sign-in button for the first time. If you have signed in before, it is set up when the app opens, so your session is restored and your syncing continues.

What leaves your device, and where it goes

  1. iCloud — to keep your record and photograph copies in your own account, as described above.
  2. Google / Firebase — for signing in and keeping your record, if you sign in. This alone reaches a server we run.
  3. Apple Maps — when a place has no coordinates, its name (such as "Amsterdam, Netherlands") is sent to Apple to find where it is. Your name, your account and your photographs do not travel with it. Souvenir does not read your device's location at all.
  4. Wikivoyage and Wikimedia Commons — when a public picture of a city is shown, the place name is sent to fetch it. Your name and your account do not go with it, and every borrowed picture is credited inside the app.
  5. souvenirtravel.app — the app downloads sight and city pictures and the price list from it. A download, not an upload: the request carries no name, no account, and nothing from your record. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, and its traffic logs are GitHub's, not ours.

As with any connection on the internet, the receiving party sees your device's address — a matter governed by those parties' own policies, which we neither see nor keep.

Information about other people

When you add travelling companions, you type their names and how they relate to you, and you may choose pictures of them. The pictures stay in your own iCloud and are not uploaded to us. The names and relations are kept with your record if you sign in — so your trips appear complete when you open them on another device. This is information about people other than you, so add those you believe would be content with it.

When you sign in on the website, and when you write to us

On souvenirtravel.app you can search, compare and wander completely freely without an account, and no record of you is kept.

When you do sign in, one line is kept with your name, email address and picture from your Google or Apple account, your interface language, and the last time you opened it. The publisher sees this line in an admin page inside the site, whose purpose is knowing that somebody signed up.

And if you send us feedback from inside the site, its text is kept along with your name, your email address and the page you were on, and the publisher reads it in that same admin page — so we can understand what you mean and reply if needed.

Children

Souvenir is not directed at children, and does not knowingly collect information about anyone.

Erasing your information

  • From the device: delete the app.
  • From iCloud: iPhone Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Souvenir → Delete Data. This removes everything the app has stored in your account, on every device.
  • From your account with us: in the app: Settings → My data → "Erase my data from the account". On the website: the "My data" page has the same button. This erases your whole space, and with it your account and your line in the sign-up register.
  • "Sign out" stops syncing at once and leaves your copy on the device exactly as it is.

If the erase does not go through — the network drops halfway, say — part of it may be gone and part may remain. The app then tells you the deletion did not finish and leaves the button in front of you to press again, and it does not erase your local copy until the deletion has actually happened. Try again whenever you like; the result is the same.

Erasing is in your hands alone, in the app or on the website, with no request to make and nothing to wait for.

Souvenir in your AI assistant

You can connect Souvenir to your AI assistant (such as ChatGPT or Claude) through our connector at mcp.souvenirtravel.app, so your assistant answers questions about destinations, visas and weather straight from Souvenir's data.

What reaches our server when your assistant asks: the question text it passes along (the month, the city, your nationality if you mentioned it) and the internet address the request came from. There are no accounts here, and nothing from your Souvenir keepsakes reaches the connector.

Two things are kept from that: a request counter keyed by the internet address, which expires after about a day and exists only for fair-use limits; and if you ask us to add a place, the text of your request, its language and its time are kept for up to ninety days, and the publisher reads it to consider adding it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes. Any material change will be explained inside the app before it takes effect.
